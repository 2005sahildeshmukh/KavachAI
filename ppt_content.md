# Kavach: pitch presentation content

Prepared for SANKALP Climate Edition, Student Track.

## Locked scope and use of this file

Kavach is a software-only, multi-hazard borrower-support platform. Heat, flood and drought scenarios share a reviewed repayment-support workflow. The proposed prototype uses twenty fictional borrowers and a mock loan system. It excludes emergency top-up loans and adaptation finance.

This file contains slide copy, visual direction, speaker notes and sources. It is not a finished PPT. The recommended structure is 10 main slides plus 2 appendix slides. This is an editorial choice, not an organiser-mandated slide count.

Status: proposed student prototype. No implementation, customer study, live integration or pilot outcome is claimed. If the team later builds a demo, update only the functions actually demonstrated. Replace bracketed team placeholders before submission.

## White-background visual direction

- Format: 16:9, white background throughout.
- Colours: charcoal text, teal headings or accents, light grey separators. Amber and red only where they carry meaning.
- Typography: one readable sans-serif family. Cover title 42–48 pt, slide titles 30–36 pt, body approximately 20–24 pt. Keep citations legible.
- Use generous whitespace and one main composition per slide. Avoid dense card grids and paragraphs on the canvas.
- A restrained 3D shield/weather image can appear on the cover. Use actual demo screenshots only after the prototype exists. Until then label visuals as concept illustrations.
- Keep schedule tables and factual diagrams editable. Put supporting detail in notes rather than shrinking slide text.

## Slide 3: Kavach's proposed workflow

### On-slide copy

**Climate evidence connected to a borrower support decision**

1. Identify exposure and preview warnings.
2. Review a qualifying observed event.
3. Show revised terms and obtain acceptance.
4. Approve, apply and confirm the change.

The lender retains control of the official loan record.

### Visual direction

A clean horizontal four-step process. Keep each step short. Use simple editable labels and connectors, without a simulated dashboard around the diagram.

### Speaker notes

A forecast can justify a warning, but it does not change a loan. A qualifying observed event opens a support case. The borrower sees the exact proposed terms and can decline. The lender approves the case and its loan system applies the change. Communication and collection records must agree with that result.

### Visual direction

An uncluttered three-row comparison with small hazard icons. Avoid a decorative map that implies precise risk modelling.

### Speaker notes

These are evidence categories for the proposed design, not validated production triggers. Flooding cannot be inferred from rainfall alone. Drought develops over a longer period than a heat event. We will demonstrate sample scenarios for all three, with clearly labelled synthetic evidence. Actual sources, thresholds and policies require validation by area and product.

## Slide 8: Funding and adoption

### On-slide copy

**Proposed buyer:** a lender serving climate-exposed borrowers

**Proposed revenue:** implementation fee and software subscription

**Relief funding:** an agreed lender budget

Pilot question: does better support justify its full cost?

### Visual direction

Three simple text blocks on a flat white canvas. Keep the funding responsibility visible. Do not add an invented revenue forecast or market-size chart.

### Speaker notes

Willingness to pay remains untested. The lender would assess software and integration costs, delayed receipts, operating work and eventual loan outcomes. Moving an instalment to maturity may delay that receipt for many months, even when the borrower gets only one payment period of immediate relief. Before any real pilot, the cohort and promised benefit need funding. We do not assume insurance or grant support will pay for it.

## Slide 9: Differentiation and validation

### On-slide copy

Existing programmes already combine climate protection with financial support and warnings.

**Kavach's proposed focus:** borrower-level repayment coordination inside a lender's workflow.

We will test:

- Time from qualifying event to confirmed support.
- Correct schedules and consistent collection messages.
- Borrower understanding and recovery after the pause.
- Full lender cost and eventual repayment outcomes.

### Visual direction

One short positioning statement and a readable measurement list. No "world first" badge and no fabricated before/after results.

### Speaker notes

CGAP's May 2026 SEWA case study describes heat protection and a broader facility linking financial services with early warnings. This means the broad category already exists. Our narrower hypothesis is that a dedicated repayment-support workflow solves a remaining operational problem. Interviews and a lender pilot must test that hypothesis. Revised due dates can mechanically lower overdue measures, so those measures alone cannot prove impact.

### Source

CGAP, Confronting the Climate and Health Nexus: Lessons from SEWA, May 2026:
https://www.cgap.org/research/publication/confronting-climate-and-health-nexus-lessons-self-employed-womens-association

## Appendix A: Essential safeguards

### On-slide copy

| Situation | Proposed response |
| --- | --- |
| Missing or conflicting evidence | Manual review |
| Borrower declines the terms | No schedule change |
| Payment arrives during approval | Refresh and review the proposal |
| Duplicate request | Return the existing result |
| Loan-system outcome is unknown | Check status before retrying |
| Business does not recover | Separate hardship review |

### Visual direction

A single editable table. Keep the wording short and the rows comfortably spaced.

### Speaker notes

These are required behaviours, not a claim that testing is complete. The mock demonstration should show the relevant states. Production also needs current-state checks at commitment, borrower data safeguards, appropriate financial treatment, group collection alignment, fair exception handling and a funded policy. A hazard index can mismatch individual losses, so an accessible review route matters.
